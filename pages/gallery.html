SWARAJYA CHRONICLES

HISTORICAL HIGHLIGHTS

As Ramchandra Pant Amatya wrote in the Ajnapatra (आज्ञापत्र), forts were the very essence of Hindavi Swarajya. From the 1645 capture of Torna Fort at age 16 to the 1674 imperial coronation at Raigad and the ocean fortifications of Sindhudurg and Vijaydurg, every Sahyadri ridge preserves three centuries of unmatched military engineering.

In 2025, twelve iconic fortresses under the Maratha Military Landscapes of India circuit gained global heritage recognition for their organic adaptation to basalt escarpments and coastal reefs.

  • ☀️Start Early at Dawn: Begin ridge ascents by 06:00 AM to beat midday heat on exposed basalt steps and enjoy crystal-clear valley panoramas.

ARCHITECTURAL & MILITARY HERITAGE

Chronicle of the Maratha Military Landscapes

How Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj transformed the basalt peaks of the Sahyadris and rocky islets of the Konkan into an unconquerable defensive network.

1. Philosophy of the Ajnapatra (आज्ञापत्र)

Authored in 1715 by Ramchandra Pant Amatya, Chapter 8 of the Ajnapatra codifies Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj's doctrine on fort administration: "The entire essence of the kingdom is its forts. If there are no forts, during a foreign invasion the land becomes shelterless and subjects are scattered."

Every fort was governed by a triumvirate of officers from different castes to prevent collusion: the Havildar (commander of the garrison and keys), the Sabnis (muster and treasury officer), and the Karkhanis (commissariat and ordinance officer).

2. Gomukhi Gateways & Machi Fortification

Maratha military architects led by Hiroji Indulkar perfected the Gomukhi (cow-mouth) gateway layout seen at Raigad's Maha Darwaza and Lohagad: flanking semi-circular bastions curve around an angled entrance so enemy war elephants could never charge the spiked teakwood doors head-on.

Below the summit citadel (Balekilla), sprawling volcanic spurs were enclosed with double-curtain walls (Chilkhati Machi) as seen on Rajgad's Sanjivani Machi.
